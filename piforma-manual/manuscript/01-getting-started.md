# Getting Started

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01; editorial-advice -->
This chapter will help you unpack your Macintosh PiForma, set up the keyboard, mouse and other accessories, and start the computer. You will also learn how to shut down properly, return everything to the bag, and keep the battery charged. The packing order matters more than you might expect.

## Unpacking the equipment

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-unpacking OWNER-TALK-2026-10-20 -->
Set the carrying bag upright on a stable surface. Open the clasp, take it out of the strap loop, and pull the loop back out through the grommets; “Closing the bag,” near the end of this chapter, shows the clasp. Leave room nearby for each item as you take it out.

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-unpacking -->
1. Remove the small brown accessory pouch near the top. It contains two game controllers, the USB hub, and the power cable. Set the pouch and its contents aside.
2. Take out the mouse and the collapsible computer stand beneath the pouch.
3. Lift out the mouse pad beneath them.
4. Remove the velvet-covered foam board beneath the mouse pad. Keep it flat; it protects the computer when everything is packed.

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-unpacking OWNER-TALK-2026-10-19 -->
The PiForma is underneath the board, with its screen facing upward. The keyboard is tucked against one side of the computer. It is not another layer in the stack. Note which way it faces, as described under “The keyboard’s place” below, and lift the keyboard out first, before the computer.

Lift the PiForma by its handle and case. Do not push on the screen: pressing on the display can damage the screen or its mounts.

## Basic ergonomics

<!-- evidence: editorial-advice -->
Choose a solid, level table with room for the stand, keyboard, mouse pad, and cables. Place the screen at a comfortable viewing angle, out of glare, with the keyboard directly in front of you and the mouse beside it. Put speakers where they will not tip, block the screen, or pull on the computer’s cables. Adjust your chair rather than spending the afternoon leaning toward the computer.

The volume dial on the keyboard does the same job as the knob on the side of the PiForma. There are two so that you can change the volume during a console game with the keyboard packed away, or reach the nearer one when the keyboard is in front of you. The nice!view display shows the keyboard’s battery level and connection; Chapter 2 explains it. You don’t need to press it.

Caps Lock deserves a warning. It is a vintage locking key, and on the PiForma it behaves like a Shift key held down. While it is down, letters come out as capitals and the number keys type symbols, and the keyboard keeps sending Shift, which runs down its battery. Make sure Caps Lock is up, level with Shift and Option, before you start and before you pack the keyboard away.

### The mouse controls

<!-- evidence: owner-current OWNER-IMAGES-2026-10-14 -->
Use the left button for an ordinary click and the wheel for scrolling. The other button is the right button. The illustration doesn’t name it, because officially the Macintosh has one button and we have never heard of a right click.

The Sync button is not needed in the normal setup: this mouse is used with its one USB wireless receiver. Leave it alone for ordinary use.

[ILLUSTRATION 1-4c|1-4c.png|The mouse buttons, scroll wheel, and Sync button. The second button is awaiting official recognition.]

Underneath are the battery compartment, power switch, and optical tracking window. The mouse runs on one AA battery. The illustration calls that last item the Eye Correction Laser. It is an optical sensor that tracks the mouse pad. It does nothing for your eyes, so keep it pointed at the desk.

[ILLUSTRATION 1-4d|1-4d.png|Mouse underside: battery compartment, power switch, and optical tracking sensor.]

The mouse can ordinarily stay on between uses, because it drops into a low-power idle state. You can still switch it off for transport or long storage. Its switch has no effect on the keyboard.

## Important safety instructions

<!-- evidence: editorial-advice; owner-current H01-safety OWNER-TALK-2026-10-20; repo-verified D-SAFETY D-HARDWARE -->
Keep liquids away, protect the display, and leave ventilation openings clear. Keep the PiForma out of direct sun. Use a stable stand on a level table. Do not carry the computer by cables or press on the screen to move it.

The power switch does not disconnect external charging power from the internal charger. Switching it off doesn’t make the inside safe to work on. Never open it up with charging power connected, and leave internal repairs to us.

Stop using the computer if it becomes unusually hot, smells hot, or has damaged power wiring or a damaged battery. Don’t keep trying to switch it on. Charge it only with its own power cable or the bag’s charging cord. It was built at home, so it carries no safety certification.

Stop using it, too, if the screen starts to flicker. A flicker can have several causes, and it needs our attention before the next session.

## Starting up the computer

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-startup -->
1. Find the power switch on the right side and switch the PiForma on.
2. Look for the green light at the front.
3. Listen for a startup chime. Depending on the rear selector’s position, there may be no sound.
4. Watch for the startup picture or sequence. The desktop normally follows in about one to two minutes; allow for some variation.

A silent startup doesn’t mean anything has failed, because some chime positions are silent. Go by the green light and the screen as well.

<!-- evidence: owner-current OWNER-TALK-2026-10-20 OWNER-IMAGES-2026-10-20 -->
The first thing on the screen is always No Signal. Don’t panic: the screen wakes up before the Raspberry Pi does, and there is no way to skip the message. It shows once, the screen goes blank, and the startup picture should appear within 10 to 15 seconds.

[ILLUSTRATION 1-6c|no-signal.png|Every startup begins with this. It is normal.]

The startup picture looks like an old Macintosh that has failed its startup test, sad face, error codes and all. That is our sense of humor. Nothing has crashed and no ROM has failed. The label at the top left names the input the screen is showing, and it should say HDMI.

[ILLUSTRATION 1-6d|boot-screen.png|The startup picture. The sad face is a joke; the label at the top left shows the HDMI input.]

If the startup picture never appears, press the source selector at the back until the screen shows HDMI. If nothing happens at all when you switch on, turn to “If the PiForma doesn’t start” at the end of this chapter.

1. Move the mouse on its pad and watch the pointer follow it. Point at an item by moving the pointer over it. To click, press and release the left mouse button once. The other left button can wait for Chapter 3.
2. Find the menu bar at the top of the screen and the folder-like AtEase launcher beneath it. If a window covers the launcher, choose View → Show Desktop from the top menu bar. This minimizes your programs into the Control Strip without quitting them.
3. In AtEase, click a folder tab if needed, then click a program button once. AtEase buttons take a single click, not a double-click. Wait for the program’s window to appear before clicking again. Use a windowed program for this first exercise; a full-screen game will cover the desktop.
4. Explore the program’s visible controls. When finished, use the program’s own Quit or Exit command if it has one, or its window close control. If asked to save something you want to keep, save it before continuing. Closing one window may leave other windows from the same program open.
5. Choose View → Show Desktop to return to the launcher. You can now open another program in the same way.

## Power for a longer session

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-power OWNER-TALK-2026-10-19; repo-verified D-HARDWARE -->
If the internal battery is charged, you do not need a power cord for a short session. A full charge typically lasts about six hours of general use, or about four hours of emulated games, especially graphics-intensive ones. For longer sessions, keep the PiForma connected to external power when it is available.

<!-- evidence: owner-current OWNER-TALK-2026-10-19 -->
The desktop has no battery gauge, and there is no low-battery warning. Check the battery display through the top slits now and then. When the battery runs out, the PiForma stops at once, just as if the power switch had been turned off, and unsaved work is lost. After a full drain, connect external power and allow a minute or two before it will start.

Take the power cable from the brown pouch. The original rear AC inlet supplies the internal charger. The battery can charge while the computer’s power switch is off. Use that cable rather than another charger.

## Stopping the computer

[SCREENSHOT 1-9|piforma-shutdown-menu.svg|Choose Shut Down from the Special menu after saving your work. This view shows the menu choice, not completed shutdown.]

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-shutdown OWNER-TALK-2026-10-20; repo-verified P-MENU P-POWER; editorial-advice -->
1. Save your work and leave any game or application using its own quit command when available. If a full-screen program gives you no way out, hold the Apple logo for about six seconds instead.
2. In the PiForma Panel menu bar, choose Special → Shut Down.
3. If a confirmation appears, choose Shut Down. Let the operating system finish shutting down before removing physical power. A dark screen can also just mean the display is asleep, so give shutdown time to finish.
4. Once shutdown is complete, turn off the power switch on the right side. The green light at the front goes out. The wiring permits battery charging while the switch is off.

Never skip that last step. Shut Down stops the software but leaves the power on, and the battery drains quickly until the green light goes out. That matters most in the bag, where you can’t see the light.

<!-- evidence: owner-current OWNER-SCREENSHOTS-1-11 -->

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-shutdown; editorial-advice -->
Switching off without shutting down first is a last resort, for a computer that won’t respond. It can lose unsaved work or damage files.

<!-- evidence: owner-current OWNER-UPDATE-1; needs-confirmation Q-GESTURE Q-SHUTDOWN -->
To leave a full-screen program rather than stop the computer, see “Leaving a full-screen program” in Chapter 3. A hold of about six seconds closes only the active application on the current PiForma.

## Packing the equipment

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-packing OWNER-TALK-2026-10-20 -->
1. Shut down properly, switch off physical power so the green light is out, and disconnect external cables as needed. Make sure the computer and accessories are cool and dry before putting them away.
2. Place the PiForma in the bag with its monitor/screen facing upward. Its rear/bottom must seat in the molded indents in the bottom foam insert.
3. Gently shift or wiggle it into its intended resting place. It should not move much when seated. Do not force it down or put pressure on the display.
4. Switch the keyboard off and check that Caps Lock is up. Put it beside the computer: keys toward the bag wall, thicker edge toward the rear, thinner edge toward the front.
5. Place the velvet-covered foam board above the computer.
6. Put the mouse pad above the board.
7. Put the mouse and collapsible stand above the pad.
8. Return the two controllers, hub, and power cable to the brown pouch and place that pouch at the top. Check that no loose item presses against the screen and close the bag gently.

## If the PiForma doesn’t start

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-troubleshooting; editorial-advice -->
Most startup trouble comes down to the battery, so begin there. Then find the symptom that matches what you see and hear. Appendix B repeats these checks in brief and covers problems after startup.

### Checking the battery

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-battery OWNER-IMAGES-2026-10-14; repo-verified D-HARDWARE D-MAINTENANCE -->
If nothing happens, check the battery before assuming something has broken. The outside plunger mechanically presses the button on the internal battery pack, showing its charge indicators; pressing it may also wake or enable the pack.

The normal, management-approved way to read the battery is to press the rear battery plunger to turn on its display, then peer through the slits in the top to see the percentage. You do not have to open the compartment for this check.

[ILLUSTRATION 1-6a|1-6b.png|Read the battery percentage through the top slits after pressing the rear battery plunger. The eye shows where to look.]

Press the plunger, read the battery, and try the power switch again. If the battery is empty, seems asleep, or won’t start despite showing charge, plug in the power cable and try again.

If it still won’t start, connect the power cable and wait roughly a minute before trying again. We’ve had this work even when the battery previously showed full. Try it before taking anything apart, but do not count on one minute as a fixed recovery time. We don’t know why it works either.

### If there is no green light

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-troubleshooting -->
If there is no green light, no chime, and no picture, start with the power switch, battery indication, plunger and power cable. Then try again. If that doesn’t help, don’t go looking inside; ask us.

### If the light is on but there is no chime

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-chime; repo-verified D-HARDWARE D-MAINTENANCE -->
Some positions on the rear chime-selector dial are intentionally silent or empty. With the PiForma powered on, move the dial through several positions in either direction and listen for an available sound.

If it never sounds, the chime module may need attention, which is a job for us. The chime has its own sound hardware, so desktop sound can still work without it.

### If the light and chime work but the screen stays blank

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-troubleshooting OWNER-TALK-2026-10-20; repo-verified D-HARDWARE D-KNOWN -->
No Signal and a moment of blank screen are normal at first. If the startup picture has not appeared after about 15 seconds, press the source selector at the back until the screen shows HDMI. The chime plays before the computer has finished starting, so wait for the picture. If the picture stays blank, flickers or distorts, ask us rather than disturbing anything inside.

### If the desktop is slow to appear

<!-- evidence: owner-current H01-troubleshooting -->
Give the computer one to two minutes to reach the desktop. If it stays on the startup picture, note what you see and tell us rather than switching it off and on repeatedly.
